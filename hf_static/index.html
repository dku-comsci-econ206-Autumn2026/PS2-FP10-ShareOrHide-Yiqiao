Strategic Disclosure in Congested Low-Altitude Drone Traffic

Research question: When competing drone operators have privately known commercial-confidentiality costs, how much flight-intent information will they strategically disclose as low-altitude traffic congestion changes, and when does the resulting equilibrium disclosure differ from the socially desirable level?

In plain language: sharing more may improve coordination, but it can also expose commercially sensitive route and timing patterns.

Evidence boundary: This Space is an exploratory classroom decision exercise. Participant choices are not a representative sample of drone operators, firms, or the public. They do not establish how real operators would behave and do not validate the model's external accuracy.

Numerical parameters are stylized normalized benchmarks rather than empirically calibrated estimates.

About / Methods

Model

  • Two commercial operators choose Minimum, Partial, or Full voluntary disclosure.
  • Private Low/High confidentiality sensitivity is independently drawn with probability 0.5.
  • The benchmark is a simultaneous pure-strategy Bayesian Nash equilibrium.
  • Social welfare is the sum of modeled utilities; disclosure-model payments do not exist.
  • The first best assumes a planner observes both realized types and may not be implementable.
  • CCAR changes only enhanced non-safety coordination access; mandatory safety information stays universal.

Privacy

No app account, name, email, student ID, database, analytics library, external API, or LLM is used. Choices and reflections remain in browser memory and disappear on refresh.

Limitations

Two operators; independent binary types; three discrete actions; pure strategies; stylized parameters; multiple-equilibrium regions outside the benchmark; omitted CCAR implementation costs; and potentially interdependent real mission values.
